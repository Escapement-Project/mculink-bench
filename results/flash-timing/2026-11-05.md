# Flash programming against SEGGER's Flasher ARM, 2026-11-05

These runs time programming a CC1312R1. Each run erases the sectors an image covers, programs the
image and verifies it. Our probe is an MCU-Link Pro running Verge `df11e14` (GCC), driven by
probe-rs. The reference is SEGGER's Flasher ARM, driven three ways: in its stand-alone mode, by
J-Flash on the PC, and by probe-rs.

One result file for each line of the tables below, matrix `flash-1`:

- `2026-11-05-ours-probe-rs-jtag.json` and `-oscan1`, `-oscan2`, `-oscan5`, `-oscan6`: our probe
  over JTAG and over each cJTAG scan format.
- `2026-11-05-flasher-standalone-crc.json` and `-readback`: the Flasher's own `#AUTO`.
- `2026-11-05-flasher-jflash-crc.json` and `-readback`: J-Flash V9.92 on the PC.
- `2026-11-05-flasher-probe-rs-jtag.json`: the Flasher through probe-rs.
- `2026-11-05-smoke.json`: single checks made before and between the sweeps.

The measuring code is `tools/flash-timing/`. Each file records the code's hashes, probe-rs
`17beb8e80`, the SEGGER software version, the probe's firmware, both images' hashes, the wiring and
the target's supply.

## Conditions

Two images, each in Intel HEX. `itm_counter.hex` is a 1,220-byte test program at 0x0.
`itm_counter_fill344k.hex` adds 344 KiB of seeded random data, from 0x2000 to 0x56000. Both stop
below the last sector, which holds the CCFG. No host was allowed to erase the whole chip: an erased
CCFG turns the debug port off from the next reset. The CCFG sector read back unchanged after every
sweep.

Every line ran at 1, 4, 10, 20, 30 and 50 MHz, five runs each. A clock that failed once is
recorded as failed, and its other runs were skipped. No analyser was attached. The tables give
medians of the good runs.

The three hosts count a run differently:

- probe-rs: from opening the probe to the core reset after verify. Verify reads the image back
  over the wire.
- The Flasher's stand-alone mode: the Flasher's own total, from connect to verified.
- J-Flash: its "Completed after" time, from connect to verified. Starting and closing J-Flash adds
  about 0.15 s, which is not counted.

Both SEGGER hosts ran twice. The first run verified with a CRC, which SEGGER computes on the target.
Its verify time stayed at 0.29 s for the large image at every clock. The second run read the image
back over the wire ("Complete data"), as probe-rs does.

## What differed between the probes

- Wiring. The Flasher's 20-pin connector reached the board's 10-pin cable through two SEGGER
  adapters, the Target Supply Adapter and the Cortex-M adapter. Our probe took the same cable
  directly. The Flasher's clock limits belong to that path. These runs do not separate the Flasher
  from its adapters.
- Supply. The J-Flash CRC line and the Flasher's probe-rs line ran on an external 3.3 V supply.
  All other lines ran from the LaunchPad's USB, with its debugger's JTAG jumpers removed. VTref
  read 3.286 to 3.289 V throughout.
- cJTAG scan format. Ours ran each format alone. SEGGER's format was not recorded on the day. A
  JLinkExe cJTAG connect recorded on 2026-10-28 selected OScan1, so OScan1 is most likely the
  comparable line.
- Small image. probe-rs writes the whole 8 KiB sector. The Flasher's program phase took 2 to 46 ms,
  which fits writing only the image's 1,220 bytes: its projects skip blank data. J-Flash's log
  reports the whole 8,192-byte range.
- Erase. SEGGER checks each sector is blank before erasing it. probe-rs does not.

## Results

With the same verify method, our probe programmed the large image in less time than the Flasher,
over both JTAG and cJTAG.

- cJTAG at 10 MHz, the Flasher's fastest working clock: the Flasher took 4.150 s and ours 3.883 s
  in OScan1. Verify alone took 1.510 s against 1.229 s.
- cJTAG, each at its fastest clock: the Flasher took 4.150 s at 10 MHz. Ours took 3.169 s in
  OScan1 and 3.138 s in OScan6, both at 50 MHz.
- JTAG at 20 MHz, the Flasher's fastest working clock: the Flasher took 3.127 s and ours 2.852 s.

With SEGGER's CRC verify, the Flasher led over cJTAG. It took 2.822 s at 10 MHz, against our
3.169 s in OScan1 at 50 MHz. Verify accounts for 0.30 s of the difference. Over JTAG the two were
level: 2.840 s for the Flasher and 2.810 s for ours, each at its best clock.

Through the same host, probe-rs, our probe was faster than the Flasher at every clock both ran.
The large image took 2.852 s against 3.729 s at 20 MHz.

Programming the large image never took less than about 1.85 s with SEGGER and 1.93 s with probe-rs.
The flash memory sets that floor. Over JTAG with CRC verify, erase took about 0.49 s with SEGGER and
0.55 s with probe-rs. With readback verify, SEGGER's erase took about 0.1 s longer.

The Flasher's path failed at 30 MHz and above over JTAG, with all three hosts, and at 20 MHz and
above over cJTAG, with both SEGGER hosts. At 30 MHz over JTAG, J-Flash read back data it had
written to target RAM with bits changed. Our probe ran every clock up to 50 MHz on every protocol.
